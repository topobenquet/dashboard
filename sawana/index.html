05

One ad is carrying the account

79 leads came from a single creative — the one comparing a 33 m² Warsaw apartment to a Bali villa. Figures in this section are Meta’s, because ad-level attribution only exists on its side: GoHighLevel records that a contact arrived, not which creative it came from. So these numbers answer “which ad works”, and the counts elsewhere in this report answer “how many leads do we have”.

Scale this

The Warsaw comparison angle is the whole campaign

79 leads at $10 each, with a click-through rate of 8.6% — roughly four times the account average. The framing does the work: it prices a Bali villa against something a Polish buyer already understands.

Worth building the next three creatives as variations on that comparison rather than new concepts — different Polish cities, different apartment sizes, same logic.

Stop spending here

The English avatar videos spent $264 for 1 lead

Across every English-language avatar video ad, $264 produced 1 lead. They earn high click-through — people watch — but almost nobody converts. For a Polish audience the language is the likely culprit, and it matches what the CRM conversations show.

The paused Carousel Renders ad tells a similar story from the other direction: $885 spent for 18 leads at $49 each — five times the cost of the winner.

Why it works

The mechanic is a price anchor, and it is carried by the text, not the picture

The first line prices 600 000 PLN as 33 m² in Warsaw, and the second line spends the same money on a furnished villa with a pool. A Polish buyer does not have to be taught what a Warsaw square metre costs, so the offer lands in one sentence. The image is only the villa — the comparison never appears in it.

That is the cheapest test available: put the comparison into the creative. A 33 m² floorplan beside the villa should amplify the mechanic that is already producing 67% of every ad-attributed lead at a quarter of the account average.

Two other things the copy does that the paused ads did not: it states the payment schedule and the projected return with an explicit “estimates, not a guarantee” disclaimer, and it dates the prices. Specific and hedged beats vague and confident for a purchase this size.

The contradiction to fix first

The ad closes with “we speak Polish” — and then the follow-up is in English

The last line of the winning ad is Rozmawiamy po polsku: we speak Polish. It is part of why the ad converts. Every lead it produces then receives follow-up messages in English.

This is a promise made in the creative and broken in the first reply, at the exact moment the lead is warmest. It is the most likely single explanation for a 10.8% lead-to-booking rate sitting under an ad with a 8.6% click-through rate.

Why the better-made ad lost

It answers the objection before it has created the desire

The carousel opens on the question it gets most from Poland — how do I manage a villa from 12 000 km away? — and spends five cards answering it: turnkey, furnished, managed, staged payments, book a call. Every card is true and useful. None of them says what the thing costs relative to anything the buyer already knows.

The single image does that in its first line: 600 000 PLN is 33 m² in Warsaw, or a villa with a pool. Reassurance is what you reach for after someone wants the thing. Sequenced the other way, it reads as an answer to a question the reader has not asked yet — which is what a 2.6% click-through rate against 8.6% looks like.

The fix is not to rebuild it. Reorder it: make the price anchor card 1, and let the current cards 1 to 4 do the reassuring behind it.

Third instance

Card 5 is a full-frame “book a call in Polish”

The closing card gives the entire frame to Umów rozmowę po polsku — book a call in Polish — and the body copy repeats it: po polsku, bez zobowiązań. Together with the winning ad, the promise of a Polish conversation is made in three separate places across the account.

Every one of those leads is then answered in English. Section 11 lists this as the second action; the evidence for it is stronger than for anything else in this report.

08

The two places the funnel loses people

Lead generation is working. What happens next is where the value is leaking, and it happens twice.

Bottleneck one

No new call has been booked in ten days

The last appointment was created on 3 August. In the ten days since, 49 leads have arrived — the strongest stretch of the campaign — and none has booked.

Overall the rate is 11 of 102 leads, or 10.8%. The pattern points at follow-up capacity rather than lead quality: bookings stopped exactly when volume tripled.

Bottleneck two

Two out of three people who book do not show up

Of the 11 appointments, 3 showed, 6 did not, and 2 are still pending — a show-up rate of 33%.

Compounded, the two bottlenecks mean 102 leads produced 3 conversations with a closer: 2.9%. Reminder sequences before the call are the cheapest fix available and typically the highest-yield.

Pipeline hygiene

81 of 92 opportunities have never moved from “New Lead”

Only 11 opportunities have advanced past the first stage — exactly the ones that booked. Everything else sits untouched, which means the pipeline cannot tell you who was contacted and who was simply missed. Recorded pipeline value is $605,000 across 92 open opportunities.

11

What we recommend

The advertising side is working and getting cheaper. Everything below the click is where the next gain is.

  1. Call every new lead within minutes, not days

    Someone considering a purchase above $150 000 expects to be treated like it, and the moment they are most willing to talk is the moment they finish the form. A call placed while the page is still open converts on a different order of magnitude to one placed the next day — and right now nothing goes out until someone gets to it.

    It does not need to be perfect coverage: agree on the hours the team can genuinely answer in, and inside those hours make the call immediate. If the lead does not pick up, that is a separate problem and the follow-up sequence handles it. The rule is that we always try, first, fast.

  2. Get WhatsApp back, and treat it as blocking

    It is the channel this audience actually replies on, and it has been down for several days — see section 10. Every day it stays down, leads arrive into a follow-up path we know underperforms. If support has not resolved it within twenty-four hours we move back to the official Meta WhatsApp channel, which has template limits but was working normally.

  3. Work the 49-lead backlog before spending another dollar

    Ten days of leads with no bookings is the single largest loss in the account. At the current cost per lead these 49 leads represent roughly $1,297 of media already paid for.

  4. Build the next creatives as variations of the Warsaw comparison

    It produces 67% of every ad-attributed lead at a quarter of the account’s average cost. Vary the city and the apartment size; keep the logic.

  5. Retire the English avatar videos

    $264 for 1 lead. That budget belongs in the Warsaw angle.

  6. Move opportunities out of “New Lead” as they are worked

    Without it, neither of us can tell a lead that was contacted and declined from one nobody ever called.
